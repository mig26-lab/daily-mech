# GXO Logistics Signs Letter of Intent for Up to 1,000 Apptronik Apollo Humanoids

![Humanoid robot in warehouse setting](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/02/06/10/13/ai-generated-8556594_1280.jpg)
*Image from Pixabay under free Content License. Source: [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/illustrations/ai-generated-robot-cyborg-animal-8556594/)*

On November 12, 2026, GXO Logistics, the world’s largest pure-play contract logistics provider, signed a letter of intent with Apptronik to expand trials of the Apollo humanoid and build a path toward deploying up to 1,000 robots over four years. The agreement uses a Robotics-as-a-Service (RaaS) model and remains contingent on the robots meeting jointly agreed performance, safety, and operational key performance indicators.

This is one of the largest conditional humanoid commitments announced in logistics to date. Expanded trials are scheduled to begin in 2027 across live warehouse workflows, with the first commercial deployments targeted for North America in late 2027 if KPIs are met.

## Scope of the Partnership

The companies plan to test Apollo on goods-to-person and person-to-goods picking, case and tote movement, packing, and sortation. These tasks go beyond simple material handling and require reliable dexterity and adaptability in unstructured environments.

Earlier collaboration provides a foundation. Apollo completed a successful U.S. pilot focused on autonomous picking of small consumer goods. Apptronik and GXO have also collected data and run live autonomous evaluations on more than 50 logistics tasks at Apptronik’s Robot Park training facility. GXO has conducted dozens of humanoid pilots across multiple vendors, giving it operational experience that informs the current framework.

Apptronik CEO Jeff Cardenas noted that the partnership demonstrates what is possible when a leading logistics provider pairs with robots designed for collaboration with people. GXO CEO Patrick Kelleher has highlighted humanoid dexterity as a transformative application of physical AI in logistics and referenced operating cost targets in the $15–20 per hour range as a marker for return on investment.

## Commercial Model and Timeline

The commercial structure is RaaS rather than an outright capital purchase of a full fleet. This allows GXO to scale robot by robot and workflow by workflow as economics and reliability improve. The full 1,000-unit figure is an ambition tied to successful trials, not a firm purchase order.

Initial North American deployment is slated for late 2027, subject to performance. Global rollout would follow over the subsequent years if targets continue to be met. The stepped approach reduces risk for both parties while creating a clear path to meaningful volume.

## Broader Context in Humanoid Logistics

The announcement arrives amid accelerating interest in humanoids for warehouse and manufacturing work. Apptronik’s Apollo 2 has also been tested with Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics 2.5 on Mercedes-Benz production lines, achieving a reported 96% success rate on parts kitting. GXO itself has expanded deployments of Agility Robotics’ Digit humanoid to additional facilities.

These parallel efforts show that large logistics operators are testing multiple platforms rather than betting on a single vendor. The combination of software advances (vision-language-action models) and hardware improvements is moving humanoids from controlled demos toward measurable operational tasks.

Challenges remain. Success rates, cycle times, uptime, and safe collaboration with human workers must hold up across diverse facilities. RaaS models transfer some financial risk but still require clear definitions of success and reliable support.

## Implications and Outlook

If the trials succeed, GXO’s network of more than 1,000 facilities worldwide offers a substantial proving ground. Positive results could accelerate adoption by other logistics and retail operators watching the economics of humanoid labor. The focus on RaaS also aligns with how many companies already consume automation services.

For Apptronik, the LOI provides a high-visibility path to scale and additional real-world data that can feed further model training. The partnership builds on prior pilots and positions Apollo for tasks that combine mobility, perception, and manipulation.

The coming year of expanded trials will be decisive. Meeting the agreed KPIs on productivity, safety, and operational integration will determine whether the 1,000-robot ambition becomes a concrete deployment plan. In the meantime, the announcement underscores that major logistics players are preparing for humanoids as a practical tool rather than a distant research project.
