# The Humanoid Robotics Surge: Deployments, Funding, and Production in Late 2026

November 2026 has seen accelerated momentum in humanoid robotics, with real-world trials, major funding rounds, production starts, and new partnerships moving the technology from research labs toward commercial and industrial use.

## Factory and Commercial Trials

Chinese company AgiBot deployed six G2 wheeled humanoids in a 24-hour live trial at a Shanghai convenience store. The robots completed 1,730 tasks, including coffee orders and shelf restocking, though two of six failed grading standards. This highlights both progress and remaining reliability challenges in unstructured retail environments.

ASUS’s Gallop humanoid has been packing motherboard components in a factory setting, targeting 99.5% accuracy within 20 seconds. Other developments include Topstar’s Star humanoid for factory sorting and UBTECH’s large order of 400 U1 Pro units for an auto dealer.

## Funding and Scaling

Agile Robots raised $500 million in a Series F round at an $8.5 billion valuation, led by SoftBank, with participation from NVIDIA and Hitachi. The funds support expansion, including in the US and Germany.

GXO Logistics signed a letter of intent with Apptronik for operational trials and a potential rollout of up to 1,000 Apollo humanoids, subject to performance milestones. Neura Robotics partnered with E.ON and enviaM to test humanoids on electricity infrastructure, with trials planned for 2027.

Tesla confirmed the start of production-quality Optimus units on high-volume lines at its Fremont factory. Figure AI has ramped production of its Figure 03 past 1,000 units.

## Specialized Advances

GENTO unveiled a lightweight 21-DoF dexterous hand (750 grams, 0.02 Nm torque), demonstrated with NVIDIA’s GR00T 2 model on two-handed tasks. Other innovations include robotic arms for EV charging (BYD) and specialized harvesters like India’s Cocobot for coconut palms.

OpenAI reportedly disbanded its dedicated robotics team, shifting focus to applying general-purpose models (such as GPT-6 Astra) directly to robot control. This reflects a broader trend where foundation models reduce the need for specialized robotics AI development.

## Outlook and Challenges

Analysts project significant growth, with some forecasts for hundreds of thousands of US humanoid units by 2030. Key bottlenecks remain in hands, actuators, compute, and reliable real-world performance. Safety, regulatory frameworks, and workforce integration will be critical as deployments expand from controlled factories to public and infrastructure settings.

The convergence of better AI models, improved hardware, and substantial capital is driving the field forward rapidly.

## Conclusion

The late 2026 humanoid robotics landscape shows tangible progress in production, trials, and funding. While full autonomy in complex environments remains a work in progress, the combination of AI advances and hardware scaling suggests accelerating real-world impact in manufacturing, logistics, and beyond.
