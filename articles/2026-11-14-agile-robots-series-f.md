# Agile Robots Raises $500 Million Series F at $8.5 Billion Valuation

![Futuristic humanoid robot concept](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/02/18/11/11/ai-generated-8581008_1280.jpg)

Munich-based Agile Robots closed a $500 million Series F funding round on November 12, 2026, valuing the company at $8.5 billion. SoftBank Group led the round, with participation from NVIDIA, Hitachi Group (including Hitachi and Hitachi Ventures), T.Capital, and others. The raise brings Agile Robots’ total invested capital to approximately $2 billion.

The company, founded in 2018 as a spin-off from the German Aerospace Center, develops humanoid and mobile robots, robotic arms, dexterous hands, and the AI software that controls them. It reports more than 20,000 robotic solutions installed worldwide across automotive, consumer electronics, healthcare, and logistics customers.

## Use of Proceeds and Production Capacity

Agile Robots plans to use the funding to expand in the United States, enter new markets, and increase production capacity, including in Germany. Earlier in 2026 the company opened a manufacturing site in Fürstenfeldbruck near Munich with the capacity to produce up to 10,000 legged and wheeled humanoid robots annually. The new capital supports scaling that capacity and accelerating application development.

CEO and founder Dr. Zhaopeng Chen has described physical AI as approaching a breakthrough in which intelligent robots work alongside people in factories and warehouses. The funding is positioned to accelerate that transition from laboratory demonstrations into industrial production.

## Investor Context and Valuation Trajectory

SoftBank previously led Agile Robots’ $220 million Series C in 2021 at a valuation above $1 billion, making it Germany’s first robotics unicorn at the time. The new $8.5 billion valuation represents substantial growth. NVIDIA’s participation is consistent with its broader investments in robotics software, simulation, and foundation models. Hitachi’s involvement aligns with prior collaboration on physical AI systems.

The round was smaller than the roughly $800 million figure Bloomberg had reported as under discussion earlier in 2026, yet the valuation places Agile Robots among the most highly valued pure-play physical AI companies in Europe. The company has also partnered with Google DeepMind to run Gemini Robotics models on its hardware, with operational data feeding back into model training.

## Market Position and Broader Robotics Funding Environment

Agile Robots distinguishes itself by combining a broad product portfolio—humanoids, mobile platforms, arms, and hands—with deep industrial roots and an existing installed base. This contrasts with some pure humanoid startups that are still primarily in pilot stages. The company employs more than 2,000 research and development specialists and holds hundreds of patents.

The raise arrives during a period of strong capital interest in physical AI. Other notable 2026 robotics funding rounds include Path Robotics’ Series E and significant commitments around humanoid deployments. NVIDIA’s simultaneous leadership transition (Jensen Huang stepping down) has not slowed its involvement in the sector; the company continues to support robotics through investments, software platforms such as GR00T and Isaac, and partnerships.

## Outlook

With expanded U.S. presence and higher production capacity, Agile Robots aims to convert its technical capabilities and customer base into larger commercial deployments. Success will depend on delivering reliable systems at scale, integrating advanced AI models effectively, and demonstrating clear economic value to industrial customers.

The $8.5 billion valuation sets a high bar. Investors and industry observers will watch whether production ramps, customer expansion, and technical progress justify the figure. In the near term, the capital provides resources to pursue the company’s stated goal of making intelligent robots a practical part of manufacturing and logistics operations worldwide.
