# Genesis AI Raises $500 Million at a $3.5 Billion Valuation to Put Its Headless Eno Robot to Work

![Illustration of a white robotic hand reaching forward against a dark circuit background](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2022/02/15/10/35/hand-7014643_1280.jpg)

*Image credit: Pixabay (image 7014643), free to use under the Pixabay Content License. Illustration of a generic robot hand, not Genesis AI's hardware.*

Genesis AI, the Paris and San Carlos, California robotics startup, said on November 17 that it has raised **$500 million in Series A funding at a $3.5 billion post-money valuation**, according to the company's [announcement on PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/genesis-ai-raises-500m-series-a-at-3-5b-valuation-to-master-human-level-dexterity-in-general-purpose-robots-and-deploy-at-scale-302941249.html). [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/french-robot-maker-genesis-raises-500-million-35-billion-valuation-2026-11-17/) called it one of the biggest funding rounds in Europe's robotics sector, as investors bet on demand for AI-enabled factory robots.

The round lands less than two years after Genesis came out of stealth with a $105 million seed, one of the largest early-stage raises in French history, per [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2026/11/17/genesis-ai-raised-500-million-to-create-human-level-robot-hands/).

## Who Is Paying

- **Co-leads:** Premji Invest, Khosla Ventures and Eclipse.
- **Other institutions:** Durable Capital Partners, T. Rowe Price, Bpifrance, HSG, NVIDIA, AMD Ventures, Zoom, Samsung Next and Ericsson.
- **Individuals:** former Google CEO Eric Schmidt, Zoom CEO Eric Yuan, Yahoo co-founder Jerry Yang, French tech entrepreneur Xavier Niel and former Tesla CFO Zach Kirkhorn.

That list, from the [company release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/genesis-ai-raises-500m-series-a-at-3-5b-valuation-to-master-human-level-dexterity-in-general-purpose-robots-and-deploy-at-scale-302941249.html), mixes chip suppliers, telecom players and late-stage public-market investors. That is a sign the bet is on deployment, not just research.

## Eno: "Human in Function, Not in Form"

Eno is not a typical humanoid. It has **no head and no face**. It is a tower of articulated panels on a rotating wheeled base, with two arms and two hands that each have **20 actuated degrees of freedom**, according to [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2026/11/17/genesis-ai-raised-500-million-to-create-human-level-robot-hands/). [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/french-robot-maker-genesis-raises-500-million-35-billion-valuation-2026-11-17/) describes three body segments "resembling oversized playing cards" that let the robot fold into a compact space and then rise to roughly human height, like a jack-in-the-box.

CEO Zhou Xian was blunt about the design choice. "I don't understand why robots need a head because ... it doesn't even have a brain living there," he told Reuters.

When Forbes saw Eno in June, its payload was about 3 to 5 kilograms per arm and battery life was around four to six hours, with both still being optimized.

## The Full-Stack Bet

Genesis builds nearly every layer itself. According to [Humanoid Mega Hub](https://www.humanoidmegahub.com/news/genesis-ai-raises-500m) and [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2026/11/17/genesis-ai-raised-500-million-to-create-human-level-robot-hands/), the stack includes:

- **GENE**, a robotics foundation model built to understand touch and dexterity. [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/genesis-ai-lands-500m-series-a-for-human-level-robot-hands) identifies the current version as GENE-26.5.
- **A 20-degree-of-freedom dexterous hand** and a **matching data-collection glove**. Genesis calls the human hand, the glove and the robot hand a "1:1:1 mapping."
- **Genesis World**, a simulator for training and testing skills virtually.
- **Eno**, the robot that applies those skills and sends deployment data back into the loop.

People wear the gloves while doing their normal jobs, and that recorded movement and touch data trains the robot directly. "Most human physical skills have never been recorded," co-founder Théophile Gervet, a former Mistral AI researcher, told Forbes.

Genesis also designs its own actuators, motors and gearboxes, while using NVIDIA chips for computing, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/french-robot-maker-genesis-raises-500-million-35-billion-valuation-2026-11-17/) reports.

## Where the Money Goes

The company lists four priorities in its [release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/genesis-ai-raises-500m-series-a-at-3-5b-valuation-to-master-human-level-dexterity-in-general-purpose-robots-and-deploy-at-scale-302941249.html):

1. Expanding data acquisition and large-scale training.
2. Solving human-level physical manipulation across many environments.
3. Deploying Eno at scale with customers.
4. Hiring more AI researchers, robotics experts and engineers.

The rollout order is industrial first (manufacturing, logistics and laboratories), then hospitality and healthcare, and finally consumer and outdoor uses.

Zhou told [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/french-robot-maker-genesis-raises-500-million-35-billion-valuation-2026-11-17/) that Genesis is prioritizing the United States first, with a US supply chain, American contract manufacturers and US customers, before expanding more broadly in Europe. It already has a partnership with South Korea's LG CNS to test Eno in manufacturing and logistics and expects a wider customer rollout in 2027.

## The Reality Check

The funding is large, but the commercial track record is still thin:

- **Small fleet.** Genesis told [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/genesis-ai-raises-500-million-to-deploy-electric-glove-wearing-robots/) it has built 30 to 40 Enos and is piloting them with a handful of undisclosed customers. A mass-manufacturing contractor is set to begin US production next year.
- **Hand durability.** Zhou told TechCrunch the company's dexterous hands can only run for a few weeks of regular work before they need replacing, and he named hardware and supply chains as the main bottleneck.
- **No demo on request.** Eno wasn't ready to demonstrate its abilities for TechCrunch during its visit.
- **Missing buyer details.** [Humanoid Mega Hub](https://www.humanoidmegahub.com/news/genesis-ai-raises-500m) notes the announcement names no customers and gives no shipment numbers, price or durability data.

## Why It Matters

- **Europe has a new robotics heavyweight.** Genesis joins Britain's Humanoid and Germany's Neura Robotics, Agile Robots and RobCo among European robotics companies valued above $1 billion, per [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/french-robot-maker-genesis-raises-500-million-35-billion-valuation-2026-11-17/).
- **Hands are the new battleground.** Locomotion is becoming a solved-enough problem. Reliable, dexterous manipulation is what decides whether a robot can do useful work.
- **Human data is the moat.** Wearable gloves give Genesis a way to collect skill data at scale without teleoperating robots one demonstration at a time, a strategy that also appears in this week's coverage of Chinese robot-training centers.
- **Form follows function.** Eno's headless, wheeled design is a bet that buyers care about throughput and reliability, not a human silhouette.

**Helpful tip:** If you are evaluating general-purpose robots for a factory or warehouse, ask vendors for three numbers before any pilot: mean time between hand or gripper replacements, task success rate over a full shift, and cost per completed task. Genesis's own comments show that hand durability is still an open question across the industry.
