# Nvidia's Leadership Earthquake: Jensen Huang Steps Down as Colette Kress Becomes Interim CEO

**Published: November 14, 2026**

![AI Technology Abstract](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2026/04/20/18/48/18-48-11-393_640.jpg)
*Image: Futuristic robotic hand with orbital light from Pixabay (free under Pixabay Content License)*

In a stunning turn of events that has reverberated across the technology and financial markets, Jensen Huang, the charismatic co-founder and longtime CEO of Nvidia, resigned on November 12, 2026, after 33 years at the helm. The board of directors announced the resignation effective immediately, naming Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO. Harvey C. Jones was appointed as the company's first-ever board chair.

This transition occurs at a critical juncture for Nvidia, which has transformed from a graphics chip maker into the dominant force powering the global artificial intelligence boom. The company's market valuation has soared into the trillions, fueled by unprecedented demand for its GPUs from hyperscalers, AI labs, and enterprises building large language models and agentic systems.

## The Circumstances Behind the Resignation

The resignation follows a series of reports detailing personal scandals involving Huang. Allegations included extramarital affairs, one with a woman identified by U.S. officials as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset associated with a major Nvidia customer, and another with a senior Nvidia executive who reported directly to him. The board had opened an investigation into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort.

Huang admitted to being unfaithful to his wife in an internal memo and apologized to family and employees. The New York Times and Reuters reported on the relationships, prompting the board to act swiftly. The timing is particularly sensitive, as Nvidia is scheduled to report fiscal results shortly after the announcement.

Market reaction was immediate, with Nvidia shares dropping significantly in premarket trading upon the news. Investors have long associated the company's success with Huang's visionary leadership, technical acumen, and relentless focus on AI infrastructure.

## Who Is Colette Kress?

Colette Kress joined Nvidia in September 2013 as CFO and Executive Vice President. Prior to that, she held senior finance roles at Cisco and spent over a decade at Microsoft, including as CFO of the Server and Tools division. During her tenure at Nvidia, annual revenue grew from approximately $4 billion to over $215 billion, coinciding with the explosive rise of AI computing.

Kress has been a steady, visible presence on earnings calls and with institutional investors. In a statement, she said: “Nvidia’s strength has always come from its people, and together with our leadership team, I am committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years.”

The board expressed full confidence in her ability to maintain operational continuity, customer commitments, and the product roadmap while a permanent successor is sought from internal and external candidates. Names mentioned in speculation include senior engineering leaders and external figures from the semiconductor industry.

## Implications for the AI Chip Race

Nvidia's dominance in AI accelerators faces intensifying competition. AMD has been aggressively expanding its Instinct MI400 series, recently adding the MI450X with 288GB of HBM4 memory and claiming superior inference throughput for its MI455X compared to Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin architecture on certain benchmarks, such as DeepSeek-R1.

Anthropic has publicly noted rapid progress in porting models to AMD hardware using AI-assisted methods. Meanwhile, custom silicon efforts from Google (TPUs), Amazon (Trainium), Microsoft, and others continue to scale. SpaceX has announced ambitious plans for multi-gigawatt AI supercomputing campuses, further underscoring the infrastructure arms race.

A leadership transition at Nvidia raises questions about the pace of innovation, supply chain management, and strategic partnerships. Huang was known for deep personal involvement in product strategy, ecosystem development (CUDA), and customer relationships. Continuity under Kress, who retains her CFO role during the interim, is expected in the near term, but longer-term vision and culture could evolve.

## Broader Industry Context

The episode highlights governance challenges in high-stakes technology companies. Personal conduct intersecting with national security concerns—particularly involving China, a major market and competitor in semiconductors—has drawn scrutiny. Export controls on advanced AI chips remain a geopolitical flashpoint.

For the AI industry, Nvidia's chips remain the gold standard for training and inference at scale. Any disruption could accelerate adoption of alternatives or open opportunities for rivals. However, the installed base, software ecosystem, and manufacturing partnerships (including with TSMC) provide significant moats.

## Looking Ahead

Nvidia's board is conducting a thorough search. Earnings results and guidance in the coming days will be closely watched for signals of stability. The AI boom shows no signs of slowing, with agentic systems, robotics integration, and sovereign AI initiatives driving demand.

This leadership change marks the end of an era. Huang's tenure defined modern AI infrastructure. The transition under Colette Kress will test whether the company can sustain its trajectory without its founder at the controls.
