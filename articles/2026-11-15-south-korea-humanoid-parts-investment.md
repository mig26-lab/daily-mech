# South Korea Commits $330 Million to Localize Humanoid Robot Core Parts, Targeting 80% Domestic Supply by 2030

**Published: November 15, 2026**

South Korea’s government has approved a major investment of 440.2 billion won (approximately $330 million) to develop and localize critical components for humanoid robots. The funding, announced around November 11, 2026, focuses on actuators, batteries, and next-generation sensors as part of efforts to build a domestic supply chain capable of competing with Chinese manufacturers in the rapidly expanding physical AI and humanoid sector.

### Breakdown of the Investment

The plan adds three core areas to South Korea’s Ultra-Innovative Economy Leading Project:

- **Actuators**: Roughly 100 billion won through 2029 to increase output from 35 Nm to 165 Nm (a fivefold improvement) and achieve durability exceeding one million cycles. Mass production of domestic actuators is targeted for 2029.
- **Batteries**: Approximately 271.4 billion won aimed at commercializing NCM and solid-state batteries that support eight hours of continuous humanoid operation by 2030–2031, including high-burst power capabilities.
- **Next-generation sensors**: About 68.8 billion won for on-sensor AI semiconductors, with demonstrations planned between 2027 and 2030.

The overall goal is to raise the localization rate of key robot components to 80% by 2030. This initiative was confirmed at an Emergency Economic Ministers’ Meeting chaired by Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il.

### Strategic Context

Humanoid robots require high-performance, lightweight actuators for joint movement, reliable long-duration batteries, and advanced sensors for perception and force feedback. Currently, many of these components are sourced from overseas suppliers, creating dependencies. South Korea, already a leader in industrial robotics density and with strong automotive and electronics supply chains (including Hyundai Motor Group and related firms), sees an opportunity to pivot suppliers into the humanoid market.

Related private-sector moves include Hyundai Mobis preparing prototype actuators for Boston Dynamics’ Atlas and broader efforts by Korean auto and IT parts makers to enter robotics. JPMorgan and other analysts have highlighted Korean firms such as Robotis as promising component suppliers as U.S. and global humanoid production scales.

### Implications for Global Robotics

Localized high-torque actuators and energy-dense batteries could lower costs and improve reliability for Korean and partner humanoid platforms. Success would strengthen South Korea’s position in physical AI alongside its existing strengths in semiconductors and manufacturing. Challenges include meeting aggressive performance targets (fivefold actuator power, multi-hour battery life under dynamic loads) and scaling from R&D to high-volume production while competing on price and quality with Chinese suppliers.
