# Agility Robotics Begins Trading on Nasdaq as AGLT After Completing SPAC Merger

**Published: November 15, 2026**

Agility Robotics, the Oregon-based developer of the Digit humanoid robot, completed its business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI and began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker AGLT (warrants as AGLTW) on November 5, 2026. The deal positions Agility as the first U.S.-listed pure-play humanoid robotics company with active commercial deployments.

### Deal Details and Capital Raised

The transaction closed on November 4 after shareholder approval on November 2. Gross capital raised was approximately $465 million before expenses, comprising:
- Roughly $164 million remaining in the SPAC trust after redemptions.
- About $201 million from a PIPE led by Foxconn at $10 per share.
- $100 million in prior equity funding that converted.

The equity valuation for Agility was around $2.5 billion. Existing shareholders and the sponsor face a 180-day lock-up (with possible early release based on price performance). First-day trading saw the stock open above the reference price and fluctuate in the $8–$12 range.

### Commercial Traction and Roadmap

Digit has been deployed in manufacturing and logistics environments with customers including GXO Logistics, Schaeffler, and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada. Reported real-world operation exceeds tens of thousands of hours, with tote-handling as a primary use case. GXO has expanded commitments to additional facilities. The company has referenced multi-year Digit 5 order commitments exceeding $300 million (subject to milestones) and is targeting expanded manufacturing capacity, including its RoboFab facility designed for up to 10,000 robots per year. Early customer access to Digit 5 is expected in the first half of 2027.

Strategic partners and backers have included NVIDIA and others. Public listing provides additional capital for scaling deployments and further development of the physical AI software stack that enables Digit’s warehouse and factory tasks.

### Significance for the Humanoid Sector

Becoming a publicly traded pure-play humanoid company increases transparency through quarterly reporting on unit economics, order backlog, and utilization. It also gives investors direct exposure to commercial humanoid deployments rather than only private startups. Success will depend on converting pilots into sustained, high-uptime operations, achieving cost reductions through volume, and demonstrating reliable performance in varied environments. The listing occurs amid broader momentum in humanoid robotics, including factory pilots by other platforms and government investments in components worldwide.
