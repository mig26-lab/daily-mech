# SpaceX Announces Over $74 Billion in AI Supercomputing Campuses in Texas and Arizona

SpaceX revealed on November 12, 2026, plans to invest more than $74 billion in two major AI supercomputing campuses—one at its Starbase facility in Texas and another in Cochise County, Arizona. The announcement underscores how central AI infrastructure has become to the company’s business, which has expanded well beyond rockets and Starlink.

The Texas project, budgeted at roughly $24 billion, will be built on SpaceX-owned land near the Port of Brownsville. The facility is designed to train frontier AI models and will generate its own electricity via an on-site gas-fired power plant rather than connecting to the public grid. Early estimates put the power plant in the 708–748 MW range. The site has already supported more than 3,000 construction jobs, with more than 500 permanent positions expected once operational. SpaceX has also committed $220 million to local water infrastructure.

The larger Arizona campus carries a planned investment of about $50 billion across roughly 747 acres. It could support up to 6 gigawatts of load at full build-out, with nine computing buildings. Unlike the Texas site, the Arizona project is expected to work with local electric cooperatives, fund transmission upgrades, and use dry cooling to avoid industrial groundwater consumption. The company projects 5,500 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent roles.

Both projects face local approvals. The Starbase City Commission is scheduled to consider a property tax abatement on December 9, 2026. In Arizona, Cochise County must approve the land-use application, and local opposition has already produced a petition with thousands of signatures. Public hearings are expected in December.

SpaceX has stated that its AI computing business now generates more than half of the company’s revenue. The firm has sold compute capacity to customers including Anthropic and has arrangements with other major AI developers. The new campuses are intended to expand this capacity significantly while giving SpaceX greater control over power and cooling—two of the biggest constraints on large-scale AI training.

The dual-site approach reflects different strategies for energy and community relations. Texas emphasizes self-sufficiency with dedicated generation. Arizona focuses on partnership with existing utilities and water-conscious design. Together they represent one of the largest single announcements of AI infrastructure investment in 2026.

For the broader AI industry, the move adds substantial new training capacity controlled by a company that already has deep ties to the frontier model ecosystem. It also highlights the growing importance of power and water in site selection for AI data centers. Dry cooling and on-site generation are becoming standard features of the largest proposed projects as grid constraints and local environmental concerns intensify.

As of mid-November 2026, neither campus has broken ground on the full-scale computing facilities. The December local votes will determine the near-term timeline. If approved, the projects would rank among the largest private AI infrastructure commitments announced to date.
