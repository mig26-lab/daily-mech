# Meta's Muse AI Agent Arrives in the UK, With Retail Partners Signed Up and Regulators Watching Closely

![Smartphone in hand showing apps](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2018/04/27/03/51/technology-3353701_1280.jpg)

*Image credit: Pixabay (ID 3353701), free to use under the Pixabay Content License. Illustrative image of a smartphone.*

Meta has brought Muse, its personal AI agent, to the United Kingdom. The launch, announced on November 24, 2026, makes Muse available to anyone 18 or older in the UK on iOS, Android, muse.ai and inside WhatsApp, according to [Meta's newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-in-the-uk-a-personal-ai-agent-you-control/). [POLITICO Europe](https://www.politico.eu/article/meta-ai-agent-muse-launch-uk/) describes it as the first AI agent product launched in the UK by a Big Tech company. That is why the country's regulators are watching it so closely.

## What Muse Does

Muse is meant to do things for you, not just answer questions. Meta says it can turn a request into a plan, work across connected apps and websites, and keep working after you close the app. It checks back when something changes or when it needs your approval.

Examples Meta gives for UK users include:

- Doing the Christmas shopping
- Searching for a cheaper energy deal
- Chasing a refund and negotiating for you
- Renewing car insurance
- Sorting a school calendar or keeping a family diary up to date
- Turning saved travel Reels into a holiday itinerary

Muse runs on **Muse Spark**, which Meta calls the first model from Meta Superintelligence Labs. Users can give their agent a name, a look and a personality. It keeps the same memory across the Muse app and WhatsApp, and users can tell it to remember or forget things. Meta says Muse is free for most uses, with optional paid plans for heavier use, and that it will come to Meta's AI glasses "in the months ahead."

"It doesn't just answer questions, it actually goes out and does work for you," David Singleton, Meta's VP of engineering on AI products, told the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9gkld2l38y6o).

## Retailers Sign On

Shopping is where Muse matters most commercially. [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/meta-launches-muse-ai-agent-in-the-uk-with-retail-partners) reports that Meta signed agreements with **Marks & Spencer, Currys, Sainsbury's and easyJet**. These deals let Muse use their apps and move through their websites more easily. If a merchant has no connector app, Muse uses its website instead. The BBC also names Debenhams Group and Ocado among the UK firms working with Meta.

The stakes are big. In the US, Muse has been downloaded millions of times and ranked first on Google's free app chart and second on Apple's, per The Paypers. One gap stands out: the BBC reports that **Amazon has blocked Muse from its marketplace**, citing data security concerns.

## The Safeguards Meta Promises

Meta calls Muse "private, safe and secure by design." According to [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/meta-launches-muse-ai-agent-in-the-uk-with-retail-partners), the company says:

- Muse can't make a purchase or send an email without the user's approval
- It connects only to apps the user authorizes
- It can't see passwords or payment methods
- Conversations are not shared with advertisers
- A separate "security agent" checks Muse's actions against the permissions the user granted

One default is drawing attention. [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/meta-ai-agent-muse-launch-uk/) notes that interactions with Muse are used to improve Meta's AI **unless users switch that setting off**.

## Regulators Got Involved Before Launch

UK regulators met with Meta before the rollout. Per [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/meta-launches-muse-ai-agent-in-the-uk-with-retail-partners):

- The **Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)** said Meta has committed to a voluntary monitoring and reporting scheme.
- The **Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)** said its talks with Meta led to changes that make the app's terms and conditions fairer, and that it will act if concerns come up.

There is wider caution too. POLITICO reports that the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum, which brings together the CMA, the Financial Conduct Authority, Ofcom and the ICO, warned last month that AI agent products "may amplify and accelerate risks" to consumers. The ICO also told the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9gkld2l38y6o) that consumers should "consider the pros and cons" before handing tasks to AI agents.

## Questions From the US Rollout

The UK launch comes days after Reuters reported that Meta staff had raised internal concerns about "undesired actions" by Muse since its US debut. According to [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/meta-ai-agent-muse-launch-uk/) and [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/meta-launches-muse-ai-agent-in-the-uk-with-retail-partners), the reported examples included:

- A staff member getting a reminder to reply to a relative's message on Signal, an encrypted messaging app
- Muse delivering a message containing random bank account balances

A Meta spokesperson called these "isolated incidents." The Paypers says most of the reported issues are understood to come from a bug that has since been fixed.

## A Reviewer's View

The BBC's technology editor, Zoe Kleinman, spent a week with Muse before launch and called it "helpful but awkward." She connected it to her personal email, calendar and phone messages. Muse found train tickets but couldn't book them through her cheaper preferred app. It also spent about an hour trying to load an Amazon page, apparently unaware it had been blocked. Muse asks permission each time it wants to do, send or post something, though users can give it more freedom.

## Why It Matters

- **Agentic commerce goes mainstream.** With major UK retailers signed on, AI agents are becoming a new layer between shoppers and stores.
- **A regulatory test case.** The UK's pre-launch engagement and voluntary monitoring commitment may shape how other countries handle consumer AI agents.
- **Platform conflict.** Amazon's block shows that big retailers may not let rival AI agents shop on their sites.
- **Privacy defaults matter.** An opt-out training setting and access to personal email and messages put the burden on users to manage what they share.

**Helpful tip:** If you try Muse, review the setting that lets Meta use your interactions to train its AI, connect only the apps you need, and keep approval prompts on for purchases and messages.
