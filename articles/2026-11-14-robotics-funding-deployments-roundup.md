# Scaling Physical AI: Funding, Deployments, and New Use Cases Drive Humanoid and Robotics Progress

Mid-November 2026 has seen a cluster of robotics announcements spanning logistics, energy infrastructure, agriculture, and specialized hardware. While individual deals vary in maturity, the common thread is movement from controlled pilots toward larger conditional commitments and new application domains. Capital continues to flow, and operators are testing robots on tasks that require both mobility and dexterity.

## Logistics Humanoids Gain Conditional Scale

GXO Logistics signed a letter of intent with Apptronik for expanded trials of the Apollo humanoid, with a path toward up to 1,000 units over four years under a Robotics-as-a-Service model if performance targets are met. Trials covering picking, packing, tote movement, and sortation are planned for 2027, with potential North American commercial deployment in late 2027. The companies have already run evaluations on more than 50 logistics tasks.

Agile Robots raised $500 million in a Series F at an $8.5 billion valuation, led by SoftBank with participation from NVIDIA and others. Proceeds support U.S. expansion and additional production capacity. The round adds to a broader pattern of large financings for physical AI companies.

Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics 2.5 has been reported at 96% success on live Mercedes-Benz parts-sorting tasks using Apptronik platforms, illustrating how vision-language-action models are being tested on real production lines.

## Expanding Beyond Warehouses

Neura Robotics partnered with E.ON and enviaM to explore humanoid robots on electricity infrastructure, with real-world trials targeted for 2027. The effort aims to apply physical AI to power-grid inspection and maintenance tasks.

In India, Kerala startup AlterSage demonstrated Cocobot, a robot designed to scale 20-meter coconut palms in about 60 seconds and harvest fruit. Commercial availability is targeted for 2027. The system addresses labor shortages in a specific agricultural niche that traditional automation has not easily served.

China’s SHAREBOT closed a financing round worth hundreds of millions of yuan and shifted branding toward robot productivity services across a multi-city network, reflecting continued growth in Robot-as-a-Service models.

Specialized hardware also advanced. GENTO unveiled a lightweight 21-degree-of-freedom dexterous hand used in NVIDIA GR00T 2 demonstrations for two-handed food-prep tasks. BYD showed a robotic arm capable of autonomously plugging high-power EV chargers, completing a 10%–70% charge in roughly five minutes in a demo.

## Software and Model Trends

OpenAI has reportedly disbanded its dedicated robotics research team, folding efforts into broader work on general-purpose models that can operate robots. This aligns with a wider industry shift toward applying frontier multimodal models to physical tasks rather than building robotics-specific stacks from scratch. Parallel research continues on harnesses and alignment methods that help unmodified models succeed at long-horizon manipulation.

## Outlook

The announcements of mid-November 2026 show humanoids and specialized robots moving into more domains while operators insist on staged, metric-driven rollouts. RaaS structures and conditional letters of intent lower the barrier for large customers to experiment at meaningful scale. At the same time, success still depends on reliable cycle times, safety around people, and economics that compete with existing labor or automation.

Funding levels and pilot results suggest that 2027 will be a year of expanded real-world testing rather than sudden ubiquitous deployment. Companies that can demonstrate consistent performance on the specific tasks operators care about—picking, inspection, harvesting, or charging—will be best positioned as the sector moves from proof-of-concept to production volumes.
