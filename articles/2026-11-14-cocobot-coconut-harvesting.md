# Cocobot: Kerala Startup's Tree-Climbing Robot Targets Commercial Launch for Coconut Harvesting in 2027

![Robot in industrial setting](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2026/03/14/02/39/tungart7-robot-10172923_640.jpg)
*Image: Humanoid-style robot in warehouse/factory concept from Pixabay (free under Pixabay Content License)*

In a development highlighting the application of robotics to traditional agriculture, Kerala-based startup AlterSage has developed Cocobot, a specialized tree-climbing robot designed to harvest coconuts. Targeting a commercial launch in 2027, the robot addresses labor shortages and safety risks in one of the world's major coconut-producing regions.

## Addressing Agricultural Challenges

Coconut harvesting traditionally relies on skilled climbers who scale tall, often slippery palm trees, a hazardous task prone to falls and injuries. Labor shortages have intensified in places like Kerala and Goa, where coconut is a significant crop. In Goa alone, thousands of hectares are under coconut cultivation, producing hundreds of millions of nuts annually, yet finding workers remains difficult.

AlterSage, founded by mechanical engineering graduate Ashin P. Krishna from Kozhikode, aims to automate this. Cocobot can scale palm trees up to 20 meters tall in about one minute. It uses a specialized robotic arm or cutting mechanism to detach ripe coconuts, and some versions include sensors to assess fruit maturity and even prune leaves or clean the tree. Operated remotely from the ground by a single person, it reduces the need for human climbers.

## Technical Features and Development

The robot is lightweight (reports suggest around 10-11 kg for portable versions) and battery-powered, with fast charging (full recharge in about 45 minutes) supporting up to 1.5 hours of continuous operation. It features tracks or gripping mechanisms suited for cylindrical tree trunks, including bent ones in some demonstrations. AI and sensors help identify mature fruit and navigate the tree.

Development received support from the Kerala Startup Mission, NABARD, and IIT Mandi’s iHub and HCi Foundation (including a reported grant of Rs 25 lakh). It won recognition at a state hackathon. Demonstrations have been held, including at Krishi Vigyan Kendra in Margao, Goa, in October 2026, where officials discussed potential inclusion in subsidy schemes. The expected price is around ₹4 lakh (approximately $4,800 USD), with government agricultural subsidies potentially making it more accessible to farmers.

AlterSage plans to extend the platform to other vertical plantation crops like arecanut, peach palm, and jucara palm, creating a broader solution for similar harvesting challenges.

## Market Potential and Outlook

With a 2027 commercial launch targeted, Cocobot represents a practical application of mechatronics and AI in agritech, tailored to local conditions rather than generalized farm robots. Success could improve harvest consistency, reduce costs over time, and enhance worker safety. For smallholder farmers, subsidies and the robot's portability are key advantages.

Challenges include scaling production, ensuring reliability in varied weather and tree conditions, battery life for larger operations, and farmer adoption. As one of the first commercially viable robots for this specific task, it could set precedents for agricultural robotics in tropical regions.

## Conclusion

Cocobot exemplifies how targeted robotics innovation can solve real-world problems in agriculture. By automating a dangerous, labor-intensive process, AlterSage is contributing to the broader wave of embodied AI applications beyond factories and into farms. As the company moves toward 2027 commercialization, its progress will be watched by agritech observers and farmers seeking safer, more efficient harvesting methods.
