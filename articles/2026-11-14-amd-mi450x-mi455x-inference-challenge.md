# AMD Instinct MI450X and MI455X: New Claims of Inference Leadership Against Nvidia

On November 12, 2026, AMD expanded its Instinct MI400 series with the MI450X, featuring 288 GB of HBM4 memory for Helios rack systems. The company also published inference results for the MI455X: 21,333 tokens per second per GPU on DeepSeek-R1, claiming up to 30% higher throughput than Nvidia’s Vera Rubin in published MLPerf offline results.

These figures come from AMD’s own testing and comparisons. The MI450X targets large-memory workloads, while the MI455X emphasizes high-throughput inference in rack-scale deployments.

## Technical Positioning

The MI450X’s 288 GB HBM4 capacity supports larger models or longer contexts without frequent offloading. Combined with Helios systems, it aims at enterprise and cloud inference where memory bandwidth and capacity matter. The MI455X results focus on tokens-per-second metrics for popular open models like DeepSeek-R1, positioning AMD against Nvidia’s latest inference-optimized silicon.

AMD has highlighted its broader Instinct roadmap, including prior generations, as it competes in the high-performance AI accelerator market dominated by Nvidia GPUs.

## Market and Competitive Context

Nvidia’s Vera Rubin and related platforms set high bars for inference efficiency. AMD’s claims, if independently validated, could expand options for hyperscalers and enterprises seeking alternatives amid supply constraints or pricing pressures. However, real-world performance depends on software stacks, optimization, power efficiency, and ecosystem support—areas where Nvidia maintains a strong lead through CUDA and extensive tooling.

Earlier reports noted AMD’s progress in both training and inference, but Nvidia’s volume and software maturity remain decisive factors for most deployments. The new numbers invite third-party benchmarks to confirm the gap.

## Implications for AI Infrastructure

Greater competition in inference accelerators can lower costs and increase availability for large-scale AI services. For robotics and edge applications that rely on high-throughput models, improved inference performance translates to faster decision loops. Enterprises evaluating multi-vendor strategies may find the MI450X/MI455X offerings relevant for specific memory-bound or high-volume inference tasks.

AMD continues to pair hardware advances with software optimizations. Sustained independent validation and customer deployments will determine whether these results shift market share or remain vendor benchmarks.

## Conclusion

AMD’s November 2026 announcements raise the stakes in AI inference hardware. The MI450X memory capacity and MI455X throughput claims provide concrete data points for comparison with Nvidia. As the industry scales, diverse accelerator options become increasingly important for both cost and supply resilience.
