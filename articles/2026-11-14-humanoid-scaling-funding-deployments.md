# Humanoid Robotics Scales: Major Funding, Logistics Commitments, and Factory Trials in November 2026

November 2026 has seen continued capital and commercial commitments for humanoid robots. Agile Robots closed a $500 million Series F at an $8.5 billion valuation. GXO Logistics signed a letter of intent that could cover up to 1,000 Apptronik Apollo humanoids. Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics 2.5 reached a 96% success rate on live Mercedes-Benz production lines. Tesla has begun producing what it calls production-quality Optimus units on high-volume lines at Fremont.

## Agile Robots Series F

Munich-based Agile Robots announced the SoftBank Group-led round on or around November 12, 2026. NVIDIA, Hitachi Group (Hitachi and Hitachi Ventures), and T.Capital participated. Total funding raised reached approximately $2 billion. Proceeds support U.S. expansion, additional production capacity (including in Germany), and new markets. The company states it has more than 20,000 robotic solutions installed worldwide and capacity at its Fürstenfeldbruck site near Munich for up to 10,000 legged and wheeled humanoids annually. Agile positions itself as a Physical AI company spanning automotive, consumer electronics, healthcare, and service customers.

## Logistics and Factory Deployments

GXO and Apptronik outlined a conditional path for operational trials and a possible rollout of up to 1,000 Apollo humanoids, subject to agreed performance measures. Separately, Apptronik’s Apollo 2 humanoids running Gemini Robotics 2.5 performed automotive kitting—picking metal brackets from bulk bins into per-vehicle sequencing trays—on active Mercedes-Benz lines in Kecskemét, Hungary, and Sindelfingen, Germany. DeepMind reported a 96% success rate. The model is described as 20–30% faster than prior versions on laboratory tasks, with improved safety completion rates around obstacles and people.

Other factory and commercial trials continue. ASUS’s Gallop humanoid has been packing motherboard components in a group factory. AgiBot G2 wheeled humanoids completed a 24-hour convenience-store trial in Shanghai, logging 1,730 tasks. Neura Robotics is partnering with E.ON and enviaM to test humanoids on electricity infrastructure.

## Tesla Optimus Production Milestone

Tesla’s Q3 2026 update stated that Optimus production began on high-volume lines at Fremont and that the units coming off the line are the first production-quality bots the company has manufactured. The company said the current manufacturing process is suitable for a commercial product while noting continued iteration. CEO Elon Musk has described the stage as early production and has spoken of significant future volumes; independent production-rate and external-customer timelines remain limited in public disclosures.

## Implications

Capital continues to flow into companies that combine hardware production with AI control stacks. SoftBank, NVIDIA, and industrial players are participating in large rounds. Logistics operators and automotive manufacturers are moving from lab pilots toward defined commercial pathways, albeit often with performance conditions. Success rates in the high 90s on structured industrial tasks are now being reported outside pure research settings.

Remaining hurdles include cost, reliability over long shifts, safety certification, and generalization beyond trained tasks. The November developments indicate that multiple independent efforts are advancing simultaneously rather than a single dominant approach. Further data on cycle times, uptime, and total cost of ownership from the GXO, Mercedes-Benz, and other pilots will clarify how quickly humanoids move from conditional commitments to sustained deployment.
