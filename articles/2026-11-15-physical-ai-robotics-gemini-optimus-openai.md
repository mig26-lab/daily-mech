# Physical AI Advances: Gemini Robotics 2.5 Hits 96% Factory Success, Tesla Ramps Optimus, OpenAI Shifts Strategy

![Humanoid robot](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/03/20/03/06/ai-generated-8644499_1280.jpg)
*Image from Pixabay: Futuristic humanoid robot illustration*

Recent days have brought concrete progress in physical AI and humanoid robotics, moving systems from laboratory demonstrations toward factory floors and production lines. Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics 2.5, Tesla’s Optimus manufacturing start, and OpenAI’s reorganization of its robotics efforts highlight different approaches to bridging digital intelligence and the physical world.

## Gemini Robotics 2.5 Factory Results

On November 10, Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2.5, its most capable vision-language-action model to date. Evaluated across more than 200 distinct tasks—including tool use, handling deformable objects such as tying bows, and delicate operations like pipetting—the model achieved 30% to 100% higher success rates than previous baselines depending on the task, while completing actions 20% to 30% faster.

In a real-world proof-of-concept on Mercedes-Benz production lines in Hungary and Germany, Apptronik’s Apollo 2 humanoid robots powered by the model performed automotive parts kitting (picking and packing metal brackets into sequence trays) with a reported 96% success rate. The system combines Gemini Robotics 2.5 for low-level control with higher-level reasoning models. DeepMind also released an open safety benchmark called ASIMOV-Jailbreak to test resistance to unsafe instructions. The model is rolling out to robotics partners including Apptronik, Agile Robots, and Boston Dynamics.

## Tesla Optimus Production Status

Tesla confirmed in its Q3 2026 update and earnings call (late October) that it has begun early production of Optimus humanoid robots on high-volume lines at its Fremont factory, in space previously used for Model S/X. These are described as the first production-quality units, and the manufacturing process is deemed suitable for a commercial product. Elon Musk indicated that reaching a target rate of around 20,000 units per week would take at least a year (possibly longer) due to the complexity of a product with roughly 10,000 components and ongoing design refinements for safety, reliability, and durability. Outside customer deliveries are targeted for around the end of 2027. Initial internal deployments are expected to focus on data collection and factory tasks.

## OpenAI’s Robotics Reorganization

On November 13, Fortune reported that OpenAI has disbanded (or reorganized) its standalone robotics team, reassigning researchers to the main research organization under Chief Research Officer Mark Chen and applied/hardware teams to the Applied organization under President Greg Brockman. The company framed the change as an acceleration of robotics work rather than a retreat, citing the ability of general-purpose models such as GPT-6 Astra to control robots directly. In a cited Nvidia study, an Astra-powered agent achieved 74.8% success on a simulated manipulation benchmark versus 51% for the best specialized robotics models. OpenAI maintains that robotics remains part of its long-term vision, with a public humanoid demo still targeted for 2027.

## Synthesis and Outlook

These developments illustrate converging trends: specialized vision-language-action models delivering measurable factory performance (DeepMind), large-scale manufacturing ramps for humanoids (Tesla), and a bet that frontier general models can increasingly handle physical control without dedicated teams (OpenAI). Challenges remain around dexterity on unstructured tasks, data collection, energy, safety, and cost. Continued factory pilots, production data from Optimus, and further model releases will determine how quickly physical AI moves from pilots to widespread deployment.
