# SpaceX Outlines More Than $74 Billion in AI Supercomputing Campuses Across Texas and Arizona

**Published: November 13, 2026**

## Introduction

SpaceX has detailed plans for more than $74 billion in investment across two major AI supercomputing campuses in the United States. Announced around November 12, 2026, the projects include over $24 billion at its Starbase site near Brownsville, Texas, and approximately $50 billion for a facility in Cochise County, Arizona.

The move underscores SpaceX’s expansion beyond launch vehicles and Starlink into large-scale AI computing. The company’s AI-related business has become a substantial revenue contributor, with customers including major AI labs.

## Project Details

| Location            | Planned Investment | Key Features                          | Jobs (approx.)          |
|---------------------|--------------------|---------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Starbase, Texas     | >$24 billion      | On-site gas-fired power generation, ~444 acres | 3,000+ construction, 500+ permanent |
| Cochise County, AZ  | ~$50 billion      | Up to 6 GW, dry cooling, co-op partnerships, ~750 acres | 5,500 construction, 1,000 permanent |

The Texas facility is designed to generate its own electricity, avoiding grid interconnection delays. The Arizona campus emphasizes dry cooling to minimize water use and partnerships with local electric cooperatives, with claims that it could reduce bills for members. Both projects face local approval processes, with key hearings scheduled for December 9, 2026.

## Broader Context and Challenges

These campuses form part of a broader race for AI compute capacity. SpaceX has already been building out data center capacity, and the scale of the new investments highlights the capital intensity of frontier AI training and inference. The Arizona proposal has drawn some local opposition, including petitions, while the Texas project involves property tax considerations.

The plans align with SpaceX’s stated goals of supporting advanced manufacturing, national security applications, medical research, and eventual space-based computing experiments.

## Conclusion

SpaceX’s $74 billion-plus commitment to AI supercomputing infrastructure illustrates the convergence of aerospace, energy, and artificial intelligence. Execution will depend on regulatory approvals, power and water solutions, and community acceptance. If completed, the facilities would represent one of the largest private AI compute expansions announced to date.
