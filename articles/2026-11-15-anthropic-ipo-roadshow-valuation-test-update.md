# Anthropic IPO Roadshow: $70–$78 Price Range Targets Up to $1.4 Trillion Valuation Ahead of Expected Nasdaq Debut

**Published: November 15, 2026**

Anthropic, the developer of the Claude family of AI models, has set a price range of $70 to $78 per share for its initial public offering of 825 million Class A shares. At the top of the range the fully diluted valuation reaches approximately $1.404 trillion. The company expects to begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT around November 19, 2026, following pricing expected near November 18. The roadshow and offering represent one of the largest tests of investor appetite for frontier AI companies.

### Offering Structure and Key Numbers

- **Shares offered**: 825 million Class A shares (majority primary, with a portion from selling stockholders including founders).
- **Price range**: $70–$78, implying a raise of roughly $57.75–$64.35 billion from the IPO itself.
- **Concurrent private placement**: Approximately $26 billion of non-voting shares to strategic investors including Google, NVIDIA, AMD, Microsoft, Broadcom, Micron, and TSMC.
- **Underwriters**: Led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley with a broad syndicate.
- **Retail allocation**: Up to about 20% expected to be available to retail investors through major brokers.

Demand has been reported as strong, with the book oversubscribed multiple times in some accounts. Revenue growth has been rapid, with recent quarterly figures in the tens of billions of run-rate revenue.

### Market and Strategic Context

The valuation is substantial but below some earlier reported targets of $1.5–2 trillion. It reflects both the explosive growth in AI demand (Claude’s enterprise and developer adoption) and investor caution after periods of market volatility and scrutiny of AI spending and returns. Strategic cornerstone investments from major chipmakers and cloud providers underscore the interdependence between frontier model labs and the hardware/software ecosystem. Founders retain significant control through dual-class or trust structures.

Proceeds are expected to fund continued model development, compute commitments, safety research, and expansion. The listing also provides liquidity for early investors and employees while subjecting the company to public-market reporting and governance expectations.

### Implications for the AI Industry

A successful Anthropic listing would further legitimize large-scale AI model companies as public-market investments and set valuation benchmarks for peers. It could influence capital allocation across the sector, from chip demand to agentic product development. Risks include execution on revenue growth, competition from OpenAI, Google, and open-source efforts, compute cost management, and regulatory or safety-related developments. The concurrent private placements highlight how strategic partners are locking in supply and alignment ahead of the public float.
