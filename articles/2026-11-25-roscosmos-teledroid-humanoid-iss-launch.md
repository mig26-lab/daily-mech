# Russia Launches Teledroid, a Humanoid Robot Built to Take Over Risky Spacewalk Chores on the ISS

![The International Space Station in orbit](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2012/11/28/11/25/satellite-67718_1280.jpg)

*Image credit: Pixabay (ID 67718), free to use under the Pixabay Content License. Illustrative image of a spacecraft in orbit.*

A humanoid robot is on its way to the International Space Station. On November 24, 2026, Roscosmos launched the **Progress MS-36** cargo spacecraft from Baikonur on a Soyuz-2.1b rocket. On board was **Teledroid**, a two-armed robotic torso designed to work outside the station so cosmonauts spend less time on dangerous spacewalks, according to [The Robotics Media](https://theroboticsmedia.com/article/russia-teledroid-humanoid-robot-launch-iss) and [SpecsLib](https://specslib.com/en/news/roscosmos-teledroid-iss-progress-ms-36-launch/). The spacecraft reached orbit as planned. Docking is scheduled for November 26.

## What Teledroid Is

Teledroid is not a walking humanoid. It is an upper-body robot made to bolt onto the station's hull, where people move by handrails, tethers and robotic arms rather than legs. Key specs reported by [SpecsLib](https://specslib.com/en/news/roscosmos-teledroid-iss-progress-ms-36-launch/) and [The Robotics Media](https://theroboticsmedia.com/article/russia-teledroid-humanoid-robot-launch-iss):

- **Degrees of freedom:** 30, across two human-like arms, dexterous hands, a torso and a sensor head
- **Mass:** About 105 kg (231.5 lb)
- **Builder:** SPA Android Technics, working with RSC Energia and TsNIIMash
- **Program origin:** A "robotic anthropomorphic multifunctional system" project started in 2021
- **Mounting:** Planned for the Nauka laboratory module, moved into place with the European Robotic Arm (ERA)
- **Environment:** Must handle vacuum, radiation and temperature swings of hundreds of degrees

Its planned jobs are the slow, risky tasks that now need a spacewalk: opening equipment casings, installing hardware, setting up worksites, pre-positioning tools, running cables, turning connectors and clipping tethers.

## How It Is Controlled

Roscosmos calls the main control method **"copy mode."** A cosmonaut inside the station, or an operator at mission control, wears an exoskeleton suit that tracks arm and hand movements in real time, and Teledroid copies them outside. External cameras send video back, and [SpecsLib](https://specslib.com/en/news/roscosmos-teledroid-iss-progress-ms-36-launch/) notes the operator workstation uses a VR headset.

Engineers also built an **autonomous mode** for repetitive, well-understood tasks, such as turning a connector or clipping a tether, so an operator doesn't have to drive every joint, per [The Robotics Media](https://theroboticsmedia.com/article/russia-teledroid-humanoid-robot-launch-iss). That mix of human control and limited autonomy reflects how robotics teams on Earth are approaching dangerous work.

## Delivered in Two Flights

Teledroid's hardware is arriving in two cargo flights, according to [SpecsLib](https://specslib.com/en/news/roscosmos-teledroid-iss-progress-ms-36-launch/):

- **Progress MS-35 (September 2026):** The operator workstation, computers and the exoskeleton suit
- **Progress MS-36 (November 2026):** The robot itself, an external observation camera and an experimental task board

[ELiA Robotics](https://eliarobotics.com/news/roscosmos-launches-teledroid-humanoid-robot-on-progress-ms-36/), citing Unite.AI, reports that cosmonauts **Pyotr Dubrov and Anna Kikina** have been setting up the workstation, practicing with virtual models and rehearsing underwater how to carry the robot outside and install it during a spacewalk.

## The Test Plan

The experiment runs in three stages, per [RobotShop's Robotics Daily](https://roboticsdaily.robotshop.com/capsules/roscosmos-teledroid-progress-ms-36-2026-11/):

1. Controlling a virtual model of the robot
2. Testing inside a sealed module
3. Working outside the station at a task board watched by an external camera

About **40 sessions** are planned: up to 20 in simulation and up to 20 inside and outside the modules. The outdoor work will use real hardware, including electrical connectors, carabiners and hand tools.

## A Mission Planned Around a Leak

Progress MS-36 also has an unusual docking plan. [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/331338/20261123/russian-humanoid-robot-launches-space-station-tuesday-cargo-ship-planned-around-leaking-tunnel.htm), citing RussianSpaceWeb's Anatoly Zak, reports that it is the first cargo mission planned to unload at one port and fire its engines at another. The goal is to avoid moving cargo through the PrK, the Zvezda transfer tunnel at the center of the station's long-running air leak. [SpecsLib](https://specslib.com/en/news/roscosmos-teledroid-iss-progress-ms-36-launch/) says the ship will first dock at the Poisk module to unload, then move to Zvezda's aft port so its thrusters line up for orbit-raising burns, with the PrK hatches kept sealed.

The total load is about 2,427 kg. Robotics Daily reports 657 kg of that is equipment and supplies for the station and its experiments, including the Teledroid system.

## Caveats

- **Nothing has been tested in space yet.** The robot has only reached orbit. Installation and every physical test are still ahead, as [ELiA Robotics](https://eliarobotics.com/news/roscosmos-launches-teledroid-humanoid-robot-on-progress-ms-36/) points out.
- **Sources are limited.** [Robotics Daily](https://roboticsdaily.robotshop.com/capsules/roscosmos-teledroid-progress-ms-36-2026-11/) notes that much of the reporting comes from Russian state media and wire copy, and launch times vary slightly between outlets.
- **Timelines are uncertain.** The installation spacewalk hasn't been publicly scheduled.

## Why It Matters

- **A new kind of space robot.** Earlier robots like NASA's Robonaut 2 worked mostly inside the station. Teledroid is designed for exterior work in vacuum.
- **Less risk for people.** Each task the robot handles is time a cosmonaut doesn't spend outside the station.
- **A proving ground for teleoperation.** Copy mode with some autonomy is the approach many Earth-based humanoid companies are using for factories and hazardous sites.
- **Lessons for future stations.** As the ISS approaches retirement, robots like this could shape how future orbital and lunar outposts are maintained.
