## Introduction

November 2026 has seen tangible advances in humanoid robotics moving from laboratory demonstrations toward real industrial environments. Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics 2.5 vision-language-action model, paired with Apptronik’s Apollo 2 humanoid, achieved a reported 96% success rate on automotive parts kitting tasks on active Mercedes-Benz production lines in Hungary and Germany.

Parallel developments include ASUS’s Gallop humanoid beginning factory packing trials and other manufacturers advancing dexterous platforms. These efforts reflect growing investment in physical AI capable of handling unstructured, real-world tasks.

## Key Developments

| Platform / Model     | Partner / Setting                  | Reported Performance                     |
|----------------------|------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Gemini Robotics 2.5 + Apollo 2 | Mercedes-Benz lines (Hungary, Germany) | 96% success on kitting metal brackets   |
| ASUS Gallop          | Electronics factory packing        | Learning by observation and simulation   |
| Various others       | Warehouses, logistics              | Expanding deployments and funding        |

DeepMind reports that Gemini Robotics 2.5 improves success rates by 30–100% on many tasks compared with prior versions and completes them 20–30% faster. The Mercedes pilot involved picking parts from bulk bins into sequenced trays, a repetitive but variable industrial task. Safety features, including inference-time obstacle avoidance, further improved reliable completion rates.

## Implications and Remaining Challenges

High success rates in controlled pilots are encouraging, yet long-term reliability, cost, cycle time, and integration with existing factory systems remain critical. Models are currently available primarily to robotics partners rather than broadly. Companies continue to emphasize that human workers shift toward higher-complexity tasks while robots handle monotonous preparation work.

Additional open-source contributions, such as Skild AI’s Steel physics engine for faster simulation, and funding rounds in the humanoid space support continued progress. Regulatory, safety, and workforce considerations will shape the pace of broader adoption.

## Conclusion

The combination of improved vision-language-action models and capable humanoid hardware is producing measurable results on live production lines. While widespread deployment is still years away for most applications, the November 2026 pilots mark a clear step from research to practical industrial testing.
