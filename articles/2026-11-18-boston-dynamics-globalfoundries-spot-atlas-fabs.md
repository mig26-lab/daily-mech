# Boston Dynamics Takes Spot Global Across GlobalFoundries Fabs and Brings Atlas Into Chipmaking

![White quadruped robot dog walking in a park](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/08/06/10/51/ai-generated-8949030_1280.jpg)

*Image credit: Pixabay – free to use under the Pixabay Content License. AI-generated illustrative image of a quadruped robot, not a Boston Dynamics Spot unit.*

Boston Dynamics and chipmaker GlobalFoundries (GF) announced on November 17, 2026 that they are expanding their robotics partnership in two directions at once: rolling out the **Spot** quadruped across GF's semiconductor fabs worldwide, and making GF the first company outside the auto industry to test the **Atlas** humanoid, according to the [Boston Dynamics press release](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-globalfoundries-expand-robotics-collaboration/).

## Spot Goes From One Site to Four Countries

Under the agreement, Spot deployments will expand across GF's manufacturing sites in:

- Malta, New York
- Burlington, Vermont
- Dresden, Germany
- Singapore

GF has run Spot at its Burlington site for more than four years. The more recent Singapore program shows why the company is scaling. Since April, two Spot robots there have traveled nearly 900 kilometers and completed more than 90,000 inspections in sub-fab areas, with a 99% mission success rate, per [Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-globalfoundries-expand-robotics-collaboration/). The sub-fab is the utility level beneath a cleanroom, packed with pumps, chillers, gas lines and electrical systems that must be checked constantly.

Using the latest hardware and software, GF also redeployed 875 routine inspections in just two weeks and says it has a road map to more than double that number using Spot's visual inspection capabilities.

"Spot has helped us shift from reactive to predictive maintenance, collecting data across thousands of inspection points to prevent downtime and keep our fabs running more efficiently," said Pradip Singh, chief manufacturing officer at GlobalFoundries.

## Atlas Leaves the Car Plant

The bigger signal is Atlas. GF will join Boston Dynamics' Atlas early adopter program and skills development workshop, which the companies call the first program of its kind for semiconductor manufacturing. Onsite testing begins in **early 2027**.

The workshop has three stated goals:

1. Evaluate potential humanoid use cases inside fabs.
2. Assess customer readiness and integration needs.
3. Begin early testing and training for specific task execution.

That work will complement GF's program to upskill its own employees in AI. As [Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/boston-dynamics-expands-robotics-partnership-with-globalfoundries/) notes, GF is the first customer outside automotive to begin testing Atlas, which until now has been tied mainly to parent company Hyundai Motor Group's factories.

"Innovative companies like GF are realizing real operational value today while collecting the kind of data that will be extremely useful toward future manufacturing innovation in the next few years," said Zack Jackowski, chief product and technology officer at Boston Dynamics.

## The Hyundai Context

The deal lands during a busy stretch for Boston Dynamics. [Just Auto](https://www.just-auto.com/news/hyundai-acquires-full-ownership-of-boston-dynamics/) reports that Hyundai Motor Group has acquired full ownership of the company to accelerate Atlas adoption. Hyundai plans to deploy 25,000 Atlas units across Hyundai and Kia assembly plants over the next few years, and Boston Dynamics is building a U.S. production facility able to produce up to 30,000 robots a year by 2028. In September, Hyundai opened its Robotics Metaplant Application Center in Georgia, where Atlas is trained on real tasks such as sequencing automotive parts.

A chipmaker customer helps Boston Dynamics show that Atlas is not just a Hyundai in-house project.

## Why Fabs Are a Good Test Bed

- **High-value uptime:** Unplanned downtime in a fab is extremely costly, which makes predictive inspection easy to justify.
- **Repetitive, data-heavy rounds:** Gauges, thermal readings, leak checks and acoustic signatures are well suited to robot routes.
- **Hard-to-staff spaces:** Sub-fab areas are cramped and noisy, and some contain hazards, so moving routine checks to robots keeps people out of harm's way.
- **Training data:** Every inspection adds to a dataset that can support future automation, a point Jackowski made directly.

The open question is what a humanoid will do in a cleanroom-adjacent environment that a wheeled or legged inspection robot cannot. The 2027 workshop is meant to answer exactly that, so expectations should stay measured until results are published.

**Helpful tip:** For facilities teams weighing inspection robots, start with a narrow set of routes where you can measure mission success rate and inspections per week, as GF did in Singapore, before expanding.
