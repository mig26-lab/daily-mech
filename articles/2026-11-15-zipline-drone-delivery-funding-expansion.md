# Zipline Secures $1.5 Billion to Scale Nationwide Drone Delivery Network

**Published: November 15, 2026**

Drone delivery company Zipline announced a $1.5 billion funding round on November 5, 2026, valuing the company at $21.5 billion post-money. The raise, led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management, and Layer Global, includes participation from Uber, Tiger Global, J.P. Morgan, ARK Invest, Fidelity, Salesforce Ventures, and others. Proceeds will expand U.S. manufacturing and hiring to build a nationwide delivery network.

### Partnership Expansions
Simultaneously, Zipline expanded its partnership with Walmart. The companies agreed to add 300 additional drone delivery sites under a multi-year deal, building on existing operations (previously around 21 sites in states such as Texas and Arkansas). Walmart has described its overall drone program (with multiple partners) as one of the largest retail efforts, with millions of deliveries completed.

Uber has also deepened ties, with goals of reaching one million daily deliveries via Zipline integration into Uber Eats by 2029 in some projections. Zipline reports strong operational metrics, including median delivery times around 19 minutes in some markets and increased share of partner delivery revenue.

### Technology and Operations
Zipline’s system includes specialized aircraft (fixed-wing and multi-rotor variants), launch/landing infrastructure, and logistics software. The company has operated internationally for years and is scaling U.S. manufacturing capacity (with facilities capable of high annual production volumes). Regulatory progress on beyond-visual-line-of-sight operations has been a key enabler after earlier limitations.

### Broader Robotics and Logistics Context
This funding reflects continued investor interest in autonomous logistics amid labor constraints and last-mile inefficiencies. It sits alongside other robotics milestones in November 2026, such as humanoid factory pilots (Gemini Robotics 2.5 on Mercedes lines, Embodied AI’s AIR), delivery robot network openings (Serve Robotics, Avride reaching one million deliveries), and industrial automation advances. Drone delivery faces remaining hurdles around regulation, noise, weather, and community acceptance, but volume and partnership data are improving the commercial case.

### Outlook
A $21.5 billion valuation and major retail commitments signal confidence that drone networks can move beyond pilots. Success will hinge on execution of the manufacturing ramp, site deployments, regulatory approvals, and unit economics at scale. For logistics and retail operators, the expanding options from Zipline, Wing, Amazon, and others increase the practicality of hybrid ground-and-air delivery strategies.
