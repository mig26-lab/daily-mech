# Meta’s Muse AI Now Places Phone Calls to US Businesses for Paid Subscribers

![AI robot with smartphone representing automated calling](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/04/30/19/06/ai-generated-8730838_640.jpg)

Meta’s consumer AI agent Muse expanded its capabilities on November 12, 2026, when Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang announced that the system can now place phone calls to US businesses on behalf of users. The feature, previously limited to early adopters who requested access during a September beta, is now available to all paid Muse subscribers in the United States. Muse, which Meta reported had surpassed 10 million users, aims to handle routine tasks that traditionally require human phone interaction.

Users can instruct Muse to contact restaurants for reservations, insurance providers, cable companies, or government agencies such as the DMV. The agent is designed to navigate interactive voice response menus, wait on hold indefinitely, complete the requested task, and return a transcript along with a summary. Meta positions the capability as a way to remove friction from everyday errands that still depend on voice channels rather than digital interfaces.

## Access and Pricing

The calling feature requires a paid subscription. Muse’s Power plan starts at $20 per month and includes 500 million tokens per week. A higher tier at $100 per month provides 3 billion tokens for heavier usage. Free users cannot access outbound calling. Meta has not publicly detailed per-call limits or exact token consumption for phone interactions, so subscribers should monitor usage. The restriction to paid plans and US businesses reflects both regulatory considerations and the need to manage costs associated with voice infrastructure and model inference.

## How the Feature Works in Practice

According to Meta’s descriptions and internal notes reported by media, Muse does not simply dial a number. It conducts the conversation, handles interruptions or transfers, and confirms completion of the request. In one publicly shared example, a Muse agent contacted a car dealership, waited for a response, and obtained the needed information while identifying itself as calling on the user’s behalf and noting that the conversation was being transcribed. The system can therefore serve as a persistent intermediary for tasks that involve waiting or multi-step negotiation.

This places Muse alongside other efforts in agentic phone interaction. Google has explored similar “Call for Me” capabilities in Pixel devices and Gemini, while startups have offered specialized calling agents. Muse’s integration into a broader personal agent that already connects to email, calendar, and other services distinguishes it by embedding calling within a persistent user context.

## Implications and Considerations

The expansion highlights the growing ability of consumer AI agents to operate across both digital and voice channels. For users, it offers time savings on hold times and repetitive inquiries. For businesses, it introduces new patterns of automated inbound calls that may require updated handling procedures, disclosure expectations, or detection methods. Questions around transparency arise: while some demos indicate the agent discloses that it is calling on behalf of a user, consistent practices will matter for trust and compliance.

Privacy and consent also warrant attention. Calls generate transcripts that Muse returns to the user, and the system must navigate regulations governing automated calling. Meta’s decision to limit the feature to paid US subscribers provides a controlled rollout while the company gathers operational data.

As agentic systems become more capable of multi-step real-world actions, phone calling represents a concrete bridge between chat interfaces and traditional services. Muse’s broader availability of this feature marks another step in making AI agents practical assistants for daily logistics rather than purely conversational tools.
