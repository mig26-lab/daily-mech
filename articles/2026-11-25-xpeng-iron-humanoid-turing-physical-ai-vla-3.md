# XPENG Debuts IRON Humanoid and Turing Physical AI Architecture, Betting That Robot "Brains and Bodies" Must Evolve Together

![Close-up of a humanoid robot face](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2016/06/24/18/38/humanoid-1477614_1280.jpg)

*Image credit: Pixabay (ID 1477614), free to use under the Pixabay Content License. Illustrative image, not an XPENG product photo.*

Chinese electric-vehicle maker XPENG used its eighth annual AI Day in Guangzhou on November 25, 2026 to show the public its advanced general-purpose humanoid robot, **IRON**, and to unveil the **Turing Physical AI Architecture (VLA 3.0)**, a shared technology stack meant to run its cars, robots, robotaxis and even flying vehicles. The event's theme was "ORIGIN," and the message was simple: the next leap in physical AI will not come from smarter models alone, but from building the model, the chips and the body as one system, according to [XPENG's announcement](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/25/3410455/0/en/xpeng-unveils-turing-physical-ai-architecture-and-debuts-advanced-general-purpose-humanoid-robot-iron.html).

## What IRON Can Do

XPENG says IRON has **76 degrees of freedom** and more than **1,500 tactile sensing points**, with touch coverage across more than 80% of key body areas. Three in-house **Turing AI chips** run its onboard models and whole-body control. [The Robotics Media](https://theroboticsmedia.com/category/humanoid-robots/today) reports the robot carries about 2,250 TOPS of onboard compute and walked onto the Guangzhou stage to interact live with the audience.

XPENG is positioning IRON as a "work partner" rather than a showpiece. Its listed capabilities include:

- Autonomous navigation
- Creating tasks from voice or app instructions
- Autonomous charging
- Reporting abnormal conditions and monitoring its own operating health

The timeline is concrete. IRON will begin training as a **store assistant in XPENG retail stores in Q1 2027**, with an official public release targeted for **Q2 2027**. [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2328104/xpeng-next-gen-iron-humanoid-robot/?zsource=newsbreak) framed the new generation as "not a toy" but a future coworker.

## Inside the Turing Physical AI Architecture

The architecture links three systems that work at different speeds, a design that echoes the "fast and slow thinking" split common in robotics research:

- **System 0 (ultra-fast):** Brings physical body control into the AI loop, coordinating power, energy, communication and actuators, and giving the AI direct feedback about the body's state.
- **System 1 (fast):** Powered by XPENG's VLA 2.0 vision-language-action model, it reads the environment and turns that understanding into immediate actions.
- **System 2 (slow):** Handles higher-level reasoning, combining vision-language task planning with an "Agent Runtime" state machine that breaks goals into steps.

Feedback from each action flows back into the loop so the system can keep adapting. XPENG also said the latest VLA 2.0 release, **version 6.4.0**, supports agent-driven closed-loop training that speeds overall training by **10 to 12 times**, and enables what the company calls recursive self-improvement and model self-distillation. Those are company claims and have not been independently tested.

"For years, the AI industry has focused on making the brain smarter. But humans do not learn through the brain alone," said He Xiaopeng, XPENG's chairman and CEO, in the [press release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/25/3410455/0/en/xpeng-unveils-turing-physical-ai-architecture-and-debuts-advanced-general-purpose-humanoid-robot-iron.html). "The brain and body must evolve together."

## The Factory Behind the Robot

To feed IRON's training, XPENG has built a **Robot Data Factory** covering more than 250 scenarios and 15 job categories, reusing data and evaluation tools from its car business. A dedicated automated assembly line runs more than 600 AI-based visual quality checks and over 1,000 tests on finished robots and key parts.

The business model is also taking shape. XPENG plans to combine hardware sales with software subscriptions and an open platform where partners can download "capability packages" and build new skills. Token-based subscriptions and scenario-based services are under exploration.

## Cars as Robots, and Beyond

The same stack runs XPENG's vehicles. Highlights from AI Day include:

- **Turing AI Co-driving** with **VLA Adaptive Deceleration**, which uses road-scene understanding to tune energy recovery. XPENG says urban congestion tests showed a 12.4% gain in usable range, with an over-the-air rollout planned for December 2026.
- A reported **13.93% reduction in accident claims** in China since VLA 2.0 rolled out.
- Public urban NGP assisted-driving test rides at its **Paris store**, with a global rollout targeted for the first half of 2027.
- A **Robotaxi** program that moved from staffed to driverless testing in eight months and received an L4 demonstration qualification in Guangzhou. Its first purpose-built robotaxi, **YOYO-01**, is due in 2027.
- Its **ARIDGE** flying-car unit has completed certification flight testing for its manned eVTOL program.

## Why It Matters

- **Carmakers are becoming robot makers.** XPENG joins Tesla and others in arguing that EV supply chains, chips and driving data give automakers a head start in humanoids.
- **Vertical integration is the bet.** Owning chips, models and hardware may cut costs and speed iteration, but it also concentrates risk in one company.
- **Retail is a cautious first job.** Starting IRON in XPENG's own stores keeps early deployment in a controlled setting before outside customers are involved.
- **The crowd is growing.** The same day, AgiBot reported its G2 humanoid sorting chips around the clock at a JCET plant in Shanghai, and Figure posted a new Figure 03 demo, per [The Robotics Media](https://theroboticsmedia.com/category/humanoid-robots/today).

**Helpful tip:** When reading humanoid announcements, separate shipped capabilities from roadmap items. For IRON, the stage demo and specs are real today; paid deployments and the public release are still 2027 goals.
