# Swiss Robotics Day 2026 Shifts Focus from Walking to Dexterous Hands and Tactile Sensing

![Futuristic humanoid robot in a high-tech lab](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2025/05/01/06/13/ai-9570488_1280.jpg)

Swiss Robotics Day 2026, held on November 13 in Zurich, highlighted a pivotal shift in the humanoid robotics race. After years of rapid progress in locomotion and balance, the event demonstrated that the next major bottleneck is manipulation: grasping, force control, and tactile feedback. Approximately 170 machines, including humanoids and robotic arms, filled the StageOne Event and Convention Center, with demonstrations centered on precise object handling rather than walking.

A defining moment occurred on the main stage when an object gripped by a robotic hand began to slip. Just before it fell, the hand adjusted its fingers based on real-time tactile information from the fingertips, catching the object. This live example underscored the theme of the day: tactile sensing and dexterity are now the critical frontiers for practical deployment.

## Key Technologies on Display

Swiss startup Inveel unveiled a robot skin only 500 micrometers thick. This single thin sensor detects pressure and contact while also sensing an object’s approach from up to 15 centimeters away. It can distinguish forces as small as 0.0005 newtons. Such sensitivity approaches the resolution needed for delicate industrial and household tasks.

Unitree founder Wang Xingxing presented a human-sized robot hand with more than 20 degrees of freedom. The design incorporates a drive structure between the fingers and palm engineered to absorb large impacts, addressing durability concerns that have limited previous high-DoF hands. Unitree also displayed its broader lineup of quadrupeds and humanoids, reinforcing its position as a high-volume producer.

Other notable systems included TACTILIA Robotics’ hands (SDH, SVH, and tendon-driven SIH models) bundled with grasp-learning software, Sonceboz actuators delivering up to 40 N of fingertip force while driving most of a 20-DoF hand, and Bota Systems’ integrated force/torque and tactile platforms. maxon showed compact integrated joints combining motor, encoder, reducer, brake, and controller.

## Industrial Implications and Challenges

The emphasis on hands reflects the practical requirements of factories, warehouses, and service environments. Walking is increasingly reliable; reliable, general-purpose manipulation is not. Hexagon Robotics highlighted the data problem: environments without public training data can require hundreds of thousands of repetitions on short cycles before a system becomes reliable.

Korean manufacturers attended specifically to explore component partnerships, signaling growing interest in specialized actuators, sensors, and transmission systems that enable these advanced hands. The event illustrated how the competitive advantage in humanoids is migrating from whole-body mobility toward the end-effector and its sensing stack.

## Broader Context

The focus aligns with concurrent developments elsewhere: Nvidia’s GR00T models demonstrating two-handed food-prep tasks, Apptronik and Agile Robots scaling production capacity, and multiple companies reporting higher success rates on factory lines when using advanced manipulation models. Tactile feedback closes the loop that pure vision-based systems cannot fully address when objects deform, slip, or present unexpected friction.

## Outlook

Swiss Robotics Day 2026 made clear that the humanoid industry has moved past the “can it walk?” phase for many platforms. The remaining technical and commercial questions center on whether high-DoF hands with sophisticated tactile sensing can be manufactured cost-effectively, remain durable under industrial loads, and integrate with general-purpose AI models at scale. Progress on these fronts will determine how quickly humanoids move from controlled demonstrations into everyday production environments.
