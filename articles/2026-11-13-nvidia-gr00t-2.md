# NVIDIA GR00T 2: World-Action Models That Learn Robot Skills from Human Video

**Published: November 13, 2026**

## Introduction

NVIDIA unveiled GR00T 2 at the Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) in Austin in early November 2026. The open world-action model (WAM) predicts both future video and robot actions jointly, enabling robots to learn dexterous skills largely from human demonstration video rather than exhaustive teleoperation.

Trained on hundreds of thousands of hours of egocentric human video plus robot data, GR00T 2 is designed for generalization across robot bodies, including parallel grippers, dexterous hands, and humanoids. Code and weights are expected in December.

## Key Performance Claims

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Zero-shot success on 50 unseen tasks | 40% |
| Success after 1 demo per task | 71% |
| Success after 5 demos per task | 95% |
| Fine-tune time for 5 demos | ~30 minutes on single RTX 5090 |
| Pretraining human video share | ~92% of data mix |

The model can transfer skills between different robot morphologies and supports agentic control where a higher-level reasoning model directs it via API. A Turbo variant runs onboard on systems such as Unitree G1 with Jetson Thor for lower-latency control.

## Training Approach and Implications

Pretraining draws heavily from open human video datasets (including a 100,000-hour subset on Hugging Face). For new tasks, a short human demonstration (via gloves, gripper, or camera) combined with robot motion data allows the model to imagine itself performing the action and generate corresponding controls. This reduces the data collection burden that has historically slowed robotics deployment.

Partners including Foxconn, Siemens, and others are evaluating or applying it for factory and industrial tasks. Independent benchmarks and full task lists remain limited in public disclosures, so real-world generalization rates will require further validation.

## Conclusion

GR00T 2 advances the shift toward foundation models for physical AI by prioritizing human video and few-shot adaptation. If the claimed success rates hold under broader testing, it could accelerate practical robot deployment in manufacturing and service settings. Developers should watch for the December weights release and associated licenses.
